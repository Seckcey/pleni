# v0.3.0 — Open Pleni from your phone with a password

**Set a password, scan a QR code, and your phone opens Pleni. On your home Wi-Fi you don't need
Tailscale, and no device needs the long key any more.** Everything new is off until you turn it on,
on the computer that runs Pleni. Pleni by **8 West Ventures, LLC**.

## What's new

- **A password for Pleni.** Set it in **Settings → Devices** on your computer. A phone, tablet or
  another computer signs in by typing it, and the browser can remember it. The key still works on
  your computer and through Tailscale.
- **Open Pleni on this Wi-Fi.** One button. Settings shows the address and a **QR code**: point
  your phone's camera at it, type the password, and you're in. No Tailscale needed at home.
- **Away from home**, sign in with the same password through Tailscale.
- **Each device gets its own pass.** **Settings → Devices** lists every signed-in device, such as
  "iPhone, Safari", with **Sign out**. A device can also sign itself out.

## How it stays safe

- **Wrong passwords slow Pleni down.** After 5 wrong tries it waits a minute, then longer, up to 30
  minutes. After 20 wrong tries in a row the password stops working until you turn it back on at
  your computer. A restart does not reset this.
- Pleni never keeps the password itself, only a scrambled form of it, in its own settings folder.
- Pleni answers only on the home Wi-Fi address you chose. At a café or hotel your laptop has another
  address, so Pleni stays closed there until you say so.
- **Phones can look, not act.** Sending messages, answering questions, merging and changing models
  work only on the computer that runs Pleni. Only that computer can set the password, turn Wi-Fi
  access on or off, or sign devices out.
- The Wi-Fi address has no padlock (it is `http://`, not `https://`), so Pleni's key never works
  there, only the password. Use it on a network you trust, like your home Wi-Fi.
- Pleni still makes no network calls of its own and has zero dependencies.

## Try it

You need Node.js 22.12 or newer.

```sh
npx pleni@latest --open   # start Pleni and open it in your browser
```

Then open **Settings → Devices**, set a password, and choose **Open Pleni on this Wi-Fi**. The first
time, Windows may ask whether Node.js may use the network: choose **Allow**.

Every change is in the [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/Seckcey/pleni/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), and
how to set it up is in the [README](https://github.com/Seckcey/pleni#open-it-from-your-phone).
